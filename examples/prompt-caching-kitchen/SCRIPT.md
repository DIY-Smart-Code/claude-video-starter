# SCRIPT — prompt-caching-kitchen

**Voice:** ElevenLabs, voice from `.env` (via `python scripts/tts.py videos/prompt-caching-kitchen`)
**Voice settings:** script defaults
**Voice direction:** Calm, warm, a developer talking to a developer. Wry on the hook, plain on the numbers, unhurried on the close.

Written for the ear: "Claude dot M D", "A P I", "slash usage". Screens keep the written forms. Every number is sourced in `fact-check.md`.

---

## Line 1 — Hook (Frame 1)

**Time:** 0 – 6s
**Delivery:** Wry. A beat before "re-reading".

    Most of your Claude Code bill isn't Claude writing code. It's Claude, re-reading.

## Line 2 — Everything, again (Frame 2)

**Time:** 6 – 16s
**Delivery:** Listing, each item its own small beat.

    Every time you send a message, Claude Code sends everything again. The system prompt. Your Claude dot M D. Every message, every tool result. Then your new line.

## Line 3 — The cold kitchen (Frame 3)

**Time:** 16 – 25s
**Delivery:** Storytelling, a little weary.

    Picture a kitchen that throws out its prep after every order. Chop the onions again. Make the stock again. Every single time.

## Line 4 — The prepped kitchen (Frame 4)

**Time:** 25 – 33s
**Delivery:** Relief. Land "already done".

    Prompt caching is the kitchen where the prep is already done. The cook keeps it, and only makes what's new.

## Line 5 — The prefix (Frame 5)

**Time:** 33 – 43s
**Delivery:** Plain, explanatory.

    Here's how. The cache matches the start of each request, the prefix. If the start is identical, it's read from cache. Only the newest turn is processed fresh.

## Line 6 — The price (Frame 6)

**Time:** 43 – 51s
**Delivery:** Matter-of-fact, stress the numbers.

    And reading is cheap. A cache read costs one tenth of normal input. Writing it costs a quarter more, once.

## Line 7 — Worked example (Frame 7)

**Time:** 51 – 62s
**Delivery:** Slow down for the two totals.

    Say your context is a hundred thousand tokens, over forty turns. Without caching, that's four million full price tokens. With caching, about five hundred fifteen thousand.

## Line 8 — Order matters (Frame 8)

**Time:** 62 – 73s
**Delivery:** Building top to bottom.

    Order matters. Claude Code stacks it from the top. System prompt and tools. Then project context. Then the conversation. Change something near the top, and everything below it gets cooked again.

## Line 9 — The new chef (Frame 9)

**Time:** 73 – 86s
**Delivery:** One item per breath.

    That's what a new chef does. Switching models. Changing effort, on most models. Turning on fast mode. Running slash compact. Upgrading Claude Code. Each one costs a slow, full price turn.

## Line 10 — Prep goes cold (Frame 10)

**Time:** 86 – 97s
**Delivery:** Even, informative.

    Prep also goes cold. With an A P I key, the cache lasts five minutes without use. On a subscription, Claude Code keeps your main conversation warm for an hour.

## Line 11 — What's safe (Frame 11)

**Time:** 97 – 104s
**Delivery:** Lighter, reassuring.

    The good news. Editing files, using skills, switching permission modes, and slash rewind all keep the cache.

## Line 12 — Habits (Frame 12)

**Time:** 104 – 113s
**Delivery:** Direct advice, three beats.

    So pick your model and effort before you start. Compact between tasks, not in the middle. And check your hit rate with slash usage.

## Line 13 — Close (Frame 13)

**Time:** 113 – 118s
**Delivery:** Warm, unhurried, final.

    Keep the kitchen warm, and you only pay to cook what's new.
